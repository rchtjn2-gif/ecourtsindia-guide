# How to check a lawyer's experience before you hire them

Choosing an advocate is one of the most important decisions in a case. Some things can be checked from public records; others only come out in conversation.

## What you can verify publicly

- **Bar Council enrolment**: an advocate must be enrolled with a State Bar Council to practise. Ask for the enrolment number.
- **Courts they appear in**: district courts, a High Court, the Supreme Court or tribunals.
- **Types of cases**: civil, criminal, family, property, company, consumer and so on.

The [eCourtsIndia advocate directory](https://ecourtsindia.com/advocate) lists advocates verified against their Bar Council enrolment, which is a quick first check.

## Reading case history responsibly

Public court records can show matters in which an advocate appeared. Use them with care:

- **Volume is not quality.** Many listings may reflect a large practice, not better outcomes.
- **Wins and losses mislead.** Outcomes depend on facts, evidence and law, not only on the lawyer.
- **Look for relevance.** Experience in your type of case and your court matters more than total numbers.

You can look up individual matters in [eCourtsIndia case search](https://ecourtsindia.com/search).

## Questions to ask in the first meeting

1. How many matters like mine have you handled, and in which court?
2. Who will actually appear at hearings: you or a junior?
3. What are the likely stages and timelines?
4. How do you charge (per appearance, per stage or a fixed fee), and what costs are extra?
5. How will you keep me updated on dates and orders?

## Red flags

- Guarantees of a particular result.
- No clear answer about fees or a refusal to give a receipt.
- Reluctance to share their enrolment details.

## What records cannot tell you

Records do not show how well an advocate communicates, how much time they will give your matter or how they negotiate. A first meeting, and references from people you trust, fill those gaps.
