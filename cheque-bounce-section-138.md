# Cheque bounce under Section 138: the time limits, step by step

A bounced cheque in India can become a criminal case under **Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881**. The law is powerful but unforgiving about deadlines: miss one step and the complaint can fail. This page sets out the timeline in plain language. It is general information, not legal advice; speak to an advocate about your own case.

## What Section 138 covers

It is an offence to issue a cheque for a debt or other liability that is returned unpaid because of insufficient funds (or because it exceeds the arranged amount), if the drawer does not pay within 15 days of receiving a written demand. The penalty is imprisonment of up to **two years**, a fine of up to **twice the cheque amount**, or both.

## The timeline

| Step | Deadline | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Present the cheque to the bank | Within its validity; RBI has limited cheque validity to three months since 1 April 2012 | Section 138(a) |
| Send a written demand notice to the drawer | Within 30 days of the bank telling you the cheque was returned unpaid | Section 138(b) |
| Drawer's time to pay | 15 days from receiving the notice | Section 138(c) |
| File the complaint | Within one month after those 15 days end (a court may accept a late complaint for sufficient cause) | Section 142(1)(b) |

The complaint goes before a Metropolitan Magistrate or a Judicial Magistrate of the first class. Where the cheque was delivered for collection through an account, jurisdiction lies with the court for the area of the payee's bank branch that holds that account (Section 142(2)(a)).

## Money during the case

- **Interim compensation:** the trial court may order the accused to pay up to **20%** of the cheque amount during the trial (Section 143A, added in 2018).
- **Deposit on appeal:** an appellate court may order a convicted appellant to deposit at least **20%** of the fine or compensation (Section 148).
- The offence is **compoundable** (Section 147), so the parties can settle.

## How big the backlog is

eCourtsIndia's analysis, [Cheque Bounce in India: Inside the Section 138 Litigation Crisis](https://blogs.ecourtsindia.com/2026/04/26/cheque-bounce-india-silent-litigation-crisis-section-138/), found that on 23 September 2026 its index held more than 1.3 crore court records citing Section 138, over 53 lakh of them pending. The government told the Lok Sabha that 43,05,932 cheque bounce cases were pending in December 2024. More than 99% of these cases sit in district and magistrate courts, Rajasthan has the most pending records, and about half of pending records were filed in 2023 to 2025.

The same post summarises the Supreme Court's 25 September 2025 directions in *Sanjabij Tari v. Kishore S. Borcar*, including dasti and electronic summons, online payment links and graded compounding costs.

## Practical tips

1. **Diary every date**: return memo, notice sent, notice received, and the 15-day and one-month windows.
2. **Keep proof of the notice** being sent and delivered.
3. **Track the case** once filed; magistrate courts list many Section 138 matters each day.
