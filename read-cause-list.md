# Reading a court cause list: a plain-English guide for litigants

A **cause list** is the court's published list of the matters it plans to take up on a given day. If you are a party to a case, it tells you whether your matter is listed, in which courtroom and roughly when.

## When it is published

Most courts publish the next day's list the evening before, and some publish supplementary lists on the day itself. Lists are usually arranged by judge or bench and by courtroom.

## The usual columns

- **Item number (serial number)**: the position of your matter in that court's list. Matters are generally called in this order, although the judge may take some out of turn.
- **Court number / hall**: the courtroom where the bench sits.
- **Case number and parties**: identifies the matter, e.g. a civil suit or writ petition, and the petitioner vs respondent.
- **Advocates**: the counsel appearing for each side.
- **Stage or purpose**: why it is listed, such as admission, arguments, evidence or orders.

## Finding your matter

Search the [eCourtsIndia cause lists](https://ecourtsindia.com/causelist) by court and date, or open your case through [case search](https://ecourtsindia.com/search) and check its next listing. Note the courtroom and item number.

## What common terms mean

- **Not reached**: the court did not get to your item that day. The matter is usually given a new date.
- **Adjourned**: the hearing was put off to another date, often at a party's request or because the bench was not available.
- **Passed over**: the matter was skipped for now and may be called again later the same day.
- **Part-heard**: hearing has started and will continue.

## Checklist for the hearing day

1. Check the final list the previous evening and again in the morning.
2. Carry your case papers and identity proof.
3. Reach the courtroom before the bench sits and confirm the item number.
4. Stay in touch with your advocate; item order can change.
5. Afterwards, check the order or next date on the case page.
