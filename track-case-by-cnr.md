# How to track any Indian court case with just a CNR number

Every case filed in a court on the eCourts system gets a **CNR (Case Number Record)**: a unique 16-character code that stays the same for the life of the case, even if the case number or court hall changes. If you have the CNR, you do not need to know the case type, year or filing number to find it.

## Where to find the CNR

It is usually printed on the case status page, on orders and on many court-issued documents, often near the top. Your advocate's office will also have it.

## Decoding the 16 characters

A CNR follows a fixed pattern, for example `DLCT01-000123-2024` written without hyphens:

| Part | Characters | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| State code | 2 letters | The state or union territory |
| District code | 2 letters | The district within the state |
| Establishment code | 2 digits | The specific court complex or establishment |
| Case serial | 6 digits | The running number of the case at that establishment |
| Year | 4 digits | The year of filing |

(The example above is illustrative, not a real case.) A fuller explanation is in the [CNR number decoded guide](https://blogs.ecourtsindia.com/2026/04/17/cnr-number-decoded-complete-guide/).

## Looking up the case and its orders

Enter the CNR in [eCourtsIndia case search](https://ecourtsindia.com/search). The case page shows the parties, court, stage, hearing history and orders; each order or judgment has its own page you can read or share.

## Keeping track of the next date

- Note the **next hearing date** shown on the case page.
- Check the [daily cause lists](https://ecourtsindia.com/causelist) the evening before to confirm the matter is listed and find its item number.
- Practices handling many matters can use eCourtsIndia's AI Clerk for hearing alerts and order summaries.

## Common mistakes

- **Typing errors**: the letters O and I are easily confused with the digits 0 and 1.
- **Using an old case number instead**: case numbers can change on transfer or registration; the CNR does not.
- **Wrong court**: a CNR identifies one establishment; a matter transferred to another court may need a fresh lookup.
